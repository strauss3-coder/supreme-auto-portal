Section 01

Executive overview

Supreme Auto is an independent premium dealership in Pretoria North, trading on hand-picked stock, straight pricing and relationships that outlast the sale. The website had to carry that reputation into a channel where most buyers now make their shortlist before they ever phone a dealership.

This is not a template. Nothing here was bought from a marketplace and re-skinned. The layout, the typography, the vehicle cards, the finance journey, the enquiry flow and the way stock is managed were all designed and written specifically for how Supreme Auto sells cars.

The result is a platform with three jobs: establish credibility in the first few seconds, let a buyer find the right vehicle without friction, and turn that interest into a conversation with the sales floor. It runs equally well on a desktop in an office, a tablet on a couch, and — where the majority of traffic actually comes from — a phone.

Section 02

The homepage experience

The homepage is a single, continuous journey rather than a set of disconnected pages. A visitor scrolls once, from first impression to enquiry, and every section along the way answers the question the previous one raised.

First impression

The page opens on a full-height hero: a showroom photograph, darkened and softly lit, with the headline set over it. The image is flagged as high priority so it is fetched before anything else — the visitor sees the dealership, not an empty box waiting to fill.

The Supreme Auto identity sits top-left as a fixed header: the SA emblem in the brand's yellow and red, a divider, then the name and Pretoria North beneath it. The emblem is served at three pixel densities so it stays crisp on a modern phone or a Retina laptop rather than softening. Navigation runs across the centre, with Contact Us and View Inventory anchored right, so the two things a buyer most wants are always one tap away.

Motion, used with restraint

Nothing moves for the sake of moving. Content fades and lifts into place as it enters view, staggered slightly so a section assembles rather than snapping in. The hero carries a gentle parallax on scroll, and a soft light follows the cursor on desktop, giving the dark surfaces a sense of depth. Headline figures count up when they reach the screen instead of sitting static.

Every one of these effects respects the operating system's reduce motion setting. A visitor who has asked their device to limit animation gets a calm, still page — the content is identical, the movement simply steps aside.

Trust, built in layers

Below the hero the page states its numbers — vehicles sold and financing partners — as animated counters, both editable by the dealership. The inventory follows immediately, because stock is the reason people come. Then the story: who Supreme Auto is, what it does, which banks it works with, how finance is arranged, and what customers have said. Contact sits last, once the case has been made.

Fixed navigation

The header stays with the visitor and condenses on scroll, so the menu and both calls to action are always reachable.

Priority imagery

The hero loads first; everything below it loads only as it is needed, so the first screen is quick even on mobile data.

One continuous scroll

No page reloads between sections. The journey from first look to enquiry never breaks stride.

Section 03

Every section, and what it does for a buyer

The site is built as one primary page with nine sections, supported by three standalone pages. This is deliberate: a buyer comparing cars should not lose their place to a page load. Below is each section and the job it performs.

Inventory

The heart of the site. Every vehicle appears as a card carrying its own photographs, year, mileage, transmission, fuel and engine, and price. Cards with several photographs become a small carousel — arrows on desktop, swipe on a phone, dots showing position — so a buyer can walk around a car without leaving the grid. Only the first photograph is downloaded up front; the rest arrive as they are needed.

Above the grid sits a seven-field filter: make, model, price band, year, body type, transmission and fuel. Results narrow the instant a choice is made — there is no search button to hunt for. Choosing a make automatically restricts the model list to that make's stock, so a buyer cannot filter their way into an empty result by accident. A single Clear Filters control resets everything.

Cars sold or reserved stay visible with a badge rather than disappearing. A busy floor is social proof; an empty one is not.

Vehicle detail

Selecting any vehicle opens a detail panel in place, showing the full photograph set with selectable thumbnails, the complete specification, and the description written for that car in the portal. From here a buyer can start a WhatsApp enquiry about that specific vehicle, with its details already filled in.

About

A short, human statement of who the dealership is, alongside a photograph of the team and a checklist of what buyers get: inspected vehicles, finance assistance, nationwide delivery. The headline statistics sit here as animated counters.

Services

Six services, each on its own card: Vehicle Sales, Trade-Ins, Vehicle Finance, Vehicle Sourcing, Warranty Options and After-Sales Support. This section exists to answer a question buyers rarely ask out loud — is this a dealership that will still take my call next year?

Financing partners

Five named institutions — ABSA Bank, WesBank, Standard Bank, MFC and Marquis Finance. Naming real banks does more for credibility than any adjective, and it pre-answers the affordability question that stops many buyers from enquiring at all.

Finance application

A five-step guided application covering personal details, employment and income, the vehicle and deposit, trade-in, and a final review. Progress is saved as the applicant works, so an interruption does not cost them the form. The last step shows exactly what will be sent before anything is submitted.

Testimonials

A carousel of real customer reviews, managed from the portal. Reviews that genuinely came from Google carry a Verified Google Review badge; those collected directly do not. That distinction is enforced by the system, not left to whoever is typing — a review cannot be dressed up as something it is not.

Section 04

How the services are presented

The six services are laid out as a responsive card grid — three across on a desktop, reflowing to two and then one as the screen narrows. Each card pairs a line icon with a short, plain description.

The icons are drawn as vectors in a single consistent stroke weight, not pulled from a stock icon pack. They scale to any size without blurring and they share a visual language with the rest of the interface, which is a large part of why the site reads as designed rather than assembled.

Cards respond on hover with a soft lift and a brightening border. On touch devices, where hover does not exist, the cards are simply legible and well-spaced — the interaction degrades cleanly instead of leaving mobile users with something that looks broken.

Why this matters commercially. Most dealership sites list stock and stop. Stating that you source vehicles to order, handle trade-ins, arrange finance and support the car afterwards reframes the business from a lot with cars on it into a dealership that manages the whole transaction. That is a materially different proposition, and it costs nothing to state.

Imagery and media

Photography carries more weight than copy on a dealership site. A buyer decides whether a car is worth a phone call from the pictures.

How images are handled

  • Uploaded by staff, resized automatically. Photographs added through the portal are scaled down before upload, so a large phone photograph does not become a slow page.
  • Loaded only when needed. Every image below the first screen waits until the visitor scrolls near it. Browsing the grid without opening a carousel costs one image per card, not five.
  • Dimensions reserved in advance. Width and height are declared on every image, so the page does not jump around as pictures arrive — a common irritation that also harms search ranking.
  • Graceful when an image is missing. If a photograph fails to load, it removes itself rather than leaving a broken icon.

Stated accurately: the site currently has no video content, no standalone photo gallery page, and no full-screen lightbox viewer. Vehicle photographs are shown through the card carousels and the detail panel's thumbnail strip. All three are straightforward additions if wanted — they are listed in Section 11 rather than claimed here.

Section 06

How a customer makes contact

There are three routes to the sales floor, and all three are recorded. The dealership sees every lead, not just the ones that happen to arrive by the channel someone is watching.

Enquiry form

Name, phone, email, vehicle and message. Saved to the dealership's inbox, then WhatsApp opens with the same details ready to send.

Vehicle WhatsApp

Started from any vehicle. The car, price, mileage and stock number are filled in automatically, and the lead is recorded first.

Finance application

The full application is emailed to the dealership; the contact details are separately recorded so the lead survives even if the email never sends.

Validation that helps rather than scolds

The form checks each field as the visitor types and clears the warning the moment it is corrected. Messages say what is wrong and how to fix it. The Vehicle of Interest field is a searchable list drawn from live stock, so it follows the floor automatically and a customer cannot enquire about a car that was sold last month.

Everything else on the contact section

  • Phone — tap to call on mobile, shown in three places across the page.
  • WhatsApp — every button on the site draws from one number held in the portal, so changing it changes all of them at once.
  • Email — opens a pre-addressed message.
  • Address and an embedded Google map, with a Get Directions button that hands off to the visitor's maps app.
  • Trading hours for all seven days, published from the portal, with closed days shown as closed.
  • Social links — Facebook, Instagram and TikTok, each hiding itself automatically if no address is set.

The deliberate design decision worth knowing. WhatsApp opens whether or not the enquiry saved successfully. If the database were ever briefly unreachable, the customer still gets through to the dealership — because reaching you is the point of the button. A failed save is logged quietly and never shown to the customer as an error.

Section 07

User experience

The guiding principle throughout: a buyer should never have to think about the website. Every decision below serves that.

Responsive across every screen

The layout is not one design squeezed into smaller boxes. It reflows deliberately at each size. On a desktop the inventory runs four cards wide with the full filter bar visible. On a tablet it becomes two. On a phone it is a single column, the menu collapses into a touch-friendly drawer, the header condenses, and the identity block scales down so it never crowds the screen. Touch targets are sized for thumbs, not cursors.

Accessibility

  • A skip to main content link for keyboard and screen-reader users.
  • Descriptive labels on every icon-only control — sixteen across the page.
  • Full keyboard operation: the finance and enquiry dialogs trap focus while open and return it exactly where it was on close. Escape closes them.
  • Text contrast tested against the dark background rather than assumed. The brand yellow reads at 14.6:1, comfortably beyond the strictest published standard.
  • Motion reduced automatically for visitors who have asked their device for it.

Typography and colour

Two typefaces, used consistently: a geometric display face for headings and the interface, a highly legible companion for body text. Sizes scale fluidly with the viewport rather than snapping between fixed breakpoints.

The palette is drawn from the Supreme Auto logo itself — the yellow and red were sampled directly from the artwork rather than approximated by eye, so the site and the signage agree. They sit against a near-black ground with restrained neutrals, which is what makes the brand colours land when they appear.

Speed

  • No heavy framework. The entire site is 176 KB of stylesheets and scripts before compression — a fraction of a typical template build.
  • The hero image is preloaded at high priority; everything else defers until needed.
  • Vehicle data is fetched once per visit and shared by every part of the page that needs it, rather than requested four separate times.
  • Successful data is cached in the visitor's browser, so a returning visitor sees stock immediately.
  • Assets are versioned, so a change reaches returning visitors instead of being masked by an old cached copy.

Search visibility

  • Structured data describing the dealership — address, phone, trading hours, location — in the format Google reads for local business results.
  • Open Graph and social preview tags, so a shared link renders as a proper card with an image rather than a bare URL.
  • A canonical address, an XML sitemap and a search-engine directives file.
  • A web manifest, so the site can be saved to a phone's home screen like an app.

Security

  • Served entirely over HTTPS.
  • Customer enquiries are stored in a private database. Only authorised Supreme Auto staff can read them — verified by testing, not assumed.
  • Access requires both a valid account and explicit authorisation. Signing in is not sufficient on its own; an account must be on an approved list that cannot be edited from outside.
  • Internal fields such as a vehicle's VIN are withheld from public view.
  • Sensitive finance-application details — ID number, home address, income — are never stored by the website. They travel only in the email to the dealership.

In closing

A digital headquarters, not a brochure

The website is where most people will meet Supreme Auto for the first time. It has been built to make that meeting count: quick to load, straightforward to use on the device the customer actually has in their hand, honest about what it claims, and secure with the customer information it holds.

Just as importantly, it is yours to run. Stock, pricing, photographs, reviews, contact details and the homepage message are all under the dealership's control. The website reflects the floor as it is today, not as it was when a developer last touched it.

What has been delivered is a foundation. The finance journey, the enquiry capture and the stock management are the parts a dealership needs first. Bookings, payments, video, marketplace syndication and the rest are extensions of the same platform, added when the business is ready for them — not a rebuild.

The measure of it. A buyer should be able to find the right car, understand what it costs, see that other people have been treated well, and reach the sales floor — in under two minutes, on a phone, without once wondering how the website works.
